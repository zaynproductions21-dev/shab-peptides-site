Claim 2 Day — Evidence Note

Assessment of the claim that Claim 2 Day is running a high daily volume of housing disrepair cases.

Compiled 21 October 2026 · all sources accessed the same day

Summary

The public record does not support the claim that Claim 2 Day is running a high daily volume of housing disrepair cases.

Claim 2 Day is not a law firm and not an authorised claims management company. Its FCA permission was cancelled on 16 November 2023 and has not been replaced. It is a set of landing pages operated by a two-person advertising agency, which collects tenant enquiries and passes them to a solicitor panel. On its own account it does not run cases at all — the files belong to whichever firms buy the leads.

Three findings carry most of the weight:

  1. Two employees, every year since 2020. The operating company's filed accounts show an average headcount of 2 in each of the last six financial years, with £363 of current assets at 31 October 2025.
  2. The "helped 1000's of people" line is template copy. The identical sentence appears on their housing disrepair, car finance and personal injury pages, with only the claim type swapped. It is not a volume figure.
  3. No visible acquisition channel. Zero Google ads, no advertiser traceable to the brand in the Meta Ad Library, and five indexed pages in Google with no ranking for any commercial search term.

Whoever passed on the "lots of cases a day" figure is most likely repeating marketing boilerplate rather than a measured number.

Regulatory status

Claim 2 Day Limited, FRN 830374, has been "No longer authorised" since 16 November 2023. The FCA Register entry carries the same company number as the Companies House record (11073137), the same Chester address (41 Hope Street, CH4 8BU), the website www.claim2day.com, and Vinod Bhardwaj as the complaints contact.

This matters because of what the sites do. Housing disrepair is one of the six claims management sectors the FCA regulates, and seeking out, referring and identifying claims is itself the regulated activity — not just conducting them. Car finance mis-selling, which they also advertise, is regulated on the same basis.

The live pages carry no FCA disclosure of any kind. There is no firm reference number, no "authorised and regulated by" statement, and no mention of the FCA anywhere in the page source except as a marketing hook on the car finance page, which is headlined "FCA LAUNCHES HUGE INVESTIGATION".

Two further points a reader should weigh:

  • The housing disrepair page runs a compensation calculator that quotes a tenant a figure such as "£1,450 – £2,400" before any person or solicitor has looked at the case.
  • It displays "Trusted by 1,000+ Tenants" and a "4.9/5 Rating" with no review platform behind either. There is no Trustpilot profile for the domain — the page returns a genuine 404, not a blocked request.

The privacy policy also cites the Data Protection Act 1998 and "the General Data Protection Regulation 2018", neither of which is the current legal framework.

Financial scale

The operating company has reported an average of two employees in every financial year since 2020, and held £363 of current assets at 31 October 2025. Two people cannot intake, qualify, evidence and place a high daily volume of housing disrepair claims, and a lead generator shipping real volume would show money owed to it by the solicitor panel.

Caveat. These are filleted micro-entity accounts, so no profit and loss account is filed and turnover is unknown. Net liabilities on their own can sit on a trading business through a director's loan. The employee count and the current-asset figure are the filed facts that bear on volume.

The housing disrepair and car finance pages are the same template with find-and-replace applied to the noun. "Helped 1000's of people" and "help people everyday" are not measurements of anything. They are the stock phrasing of the page, reused across three sectors the operator is not authorised to work in.

The newest housing disrepair page, housingdisrepair.claim2day.com, adds a further set of claims that cannot be checked: three named testimonials quoting exact payouts of £4,200, £3,100 and £2,800, attributed only to first names and cities.

Paid marketing footprint

No advertising spend is publicly disclosed for UK commercial advertisers, so no exact figure exists for them or for anyone else in this market. What can be checked:

  • Google: nothing. The Ads Transparency Centre returns zero ads for claim2day.com across all time in the UK. There is no Google Ads tag, GA4 property or Tag Manager container on any of their pages — they are not even instrumented to measure Google conversions.
  • Meta: a pixel, no traceable advertiser. The housing disrepair page fires Meta Pixel 1998601277516551, but no advertiser under this brand appears in the Meta Ad Library for the UK. If they are buying, it is through unbranded pages.

That second point is worth care. The UK housing disrepair category in the Ad Library runs to roughly 7,600 results and is dominated by generic, brand-free pages — Support for Housing, Housing Assessment Support, Right2Housing, Claims Helpline, Justice For Mums. Several already carry the notice that their ads were removed for breaching Meta's advertising standards. This is the pattern an unauthorised operator would use, but no specific page could be tied to Claim 2 Day, and this note does not assert that any of those pages is theirs.

The stack itself is lightweight: AI-generated landing pages on a no-code builder, with every form posting to a single GoHighLevel CRM account. The dorsethousing subdomain suggests per-area or per-buyer pages, which is consistent with selling the same enquiries to more than one firm.

They rank first for their own brand name and nothing else. The second result for that brand query is their Companies House record.

Their newest and most polished page, housingdisrepair.claim2day.com, is not indexed by Google at all.

The reasons are structural rather than competitive. The parent domain is a holding page, so there is no authority to pass to the subdomains. Each funnel is a single orphan page with no internal links. There is no sitemap. The newest page renders its content in the browser rather than in the HTML, and sets a canonical tag pointing at the site root. There is nothing for a search engine to match a query against other than a form.

Method note. These are live search results from a single UK location on one date, not tracked rank data, and positions move. Absence from the top 20 across six commercial terms is not a position that moves.

What this means in practice

Treat the volume figure as marketing copy until someone produces a source for it. Ask whoever quoted it where the number came from. If the answer is the website, the relevant fact is that the same sentence appears on their car finance and personal injury pages.

Two things are worth separating. Whether Claim 2 Day generates meaningful lead volume is unknown and unknowable from outside — they could be buying paid social under pages that cannot be traced to them. Whether they run "lots of cases" is a different question, and the answer is that they run none: they are a lead generator passing enquiries to a solicitor panel, and have been unauthorised to do even that since November 2023.

On whether to report it, the facts that would support a referral are documented and dated:

  • Unauthorised claims management activity in two FCA-regulated sectors, housing disrepair and car finance, since 16 November 2023 — reportable to the FCA through its unauthorised business reporting form.
  • Unsubstantiated performance and rating claims, and a compensation estimate given before any case assessment — within the ASA's remit.

That is a judgement call for the firm rather than a recommendation in this note.

Open question. It has not been established how long the car finance and personal injury pages have been live, or whether any solicitor firm is currently accepting referrals from them. Both are worth knowing before this note goes any further than the person who raised it.
